Many teens in Singapore struggle with sleep, screen time, and communicating openly with their parents. Traditional health education often feels prescriptive and disconnected from their daily lives.

HPB's Youth Peer Support programme needed something different: tools that teens would actually want to use — not because they had to, but because the experience felt relevant, fun, and peer-driven.

Our approach was to design physical, tactile prototypes that bring parents and teens into shared activities — lowering the barrier to connection through play, routine, and honest conversation.

Concept 01

Lights Out,
Level Up

A magnetic board you and your parent use to build a bedtime routine together. Stick it on the fridge, pick your magnets, and track how you sleep.

  • Drag-and-drop magnets for wind-down activities and room setup
  • A dedicated parent column for shared commitment
  • Weekly sleep tracker with feeling magnets (Calm, Sad, Happy, Angry)
  • Designed for the fridge — visible, physical, and part of daily life
Concept 02

Hook U Up

A 14-day challenge deck you do with a friend. Pick a topic, flip a card each day, and prove you did it before midnight. Miss a day and the streak resets.

  • 5 themed decks: Bulk Up, Cut Season, Energy Era, Glow Up, Recharge
  • 14 daily challenge cards per deck with escalating difficulty
  • Photo proof required before midnight — accountability with a buddy
  • Complete all 14 days for a chance at an HPB prize
Concept 03

We Open Up
(WOU)

A card game for you and your parent. Draw a question, both answer honestly, and turn awkward silence into real conversation — one question at a time.

  • Category Cards — everyone answers, no skipping (yes, parents too)
  • Swap Cards — answer as the other person, then compare
  • Spark Cards — go one layer deeper, completely optional
  • No right answers, no judgement — just honest conversation
Design Process

How we got here.

From research to physical prototypes — a youth-centred, iterative design process.

Step 01

Research & Discovery

Understanding teen-parent dynamics, sleep behaviours, and peer influence through interviews and secondary research with HPB.

Step 02

Concept Development

Ideating three distinct formats — a board, challenge cards, and a conversation game — each targeting different aspects of teen wellness.

Step 03

Prototyping

Designing high-fidelity physical prototypes with illustrated magnets, printed card decks, and interactive digital companions for testing.

Step 04

User Testing

Testing prototypes with real teen-parent pairs, observing interactions, and iterating based on what resonated and what fell flat.
